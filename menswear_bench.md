# The bench — brands considered and not seated

Rebuilt 13 September 2026. Supersedes the version of 30 August, whose three entries are carried
below unchanged.

**What the bench is for.** A brand here has been looked at and not seated. That is a finding, and the
reasoning is the point: without it, the next person to notice the brand's absence has to redo the
thinking, and a brand deliberately excluded reads identically to one nobody considered. Same doctrine
as the rest of the project — a default is a claim.

**What the bench is not.** It is not an inventory of every brand encountered. That is
`seen_register_2026-09-13.csv`, which holds 827 names carried by shops in the register and never
assessed, with a count of how many shops carry each. **The bench holds judgements; the seen register
holds sightings.** A name moves from one to the other when somebody looks at it.

**What seating requires.** Origin, the dials, staple prices, a one thing, and a US own-store position.
A brand can sit here indefinitely without that being a failure.

## Benched after assessment

### Club Monaco
**Unseated** 22 September 2026 · was seated, Accessible Luxury, 13 US stores
The men's range is winding down: on the 21 September link read the men's pants page held one product,
sold out and final sale, and the knitwear page one. A house with no men's rail to send a reader to
cannot hold a seat. **What would change it:** a men's range that exists again on its own store.


### Autumn Cashmere
**Benched** September 2026 · **Seen in** 3 register shops
Trades heavily through off-price. The map's price bands describe full-price US retail, and a house
whose real selling price is a markdown would sit in the wrong band whichever figure was taken.
**What would change it:** evidence that its full-price channel is the dominant one.

### Marco Pescarolo
**Held** 13 September 2026 · captured, not seated
Neapolitan trouser house, Caivano, 1999. **No e-commerce of any kind**, so seven price slots and six
fibre counts do not exist to be captured — a structural absence rather than a shortfall. Everything
the house publishes about itself is captured and the row is complete on those columns.
**What would change it:** a US retail source that is the house's own, not a stockist's markup.

### Lardini
**Held** 13 September 2026 · one pass short
Filottrano tailoring house. Prices, fibre, stockists and the one thing are all captured; four
narrative columns — founding date, people, ownership — sit on three history pages identified but not
read. A founding date was available from a third party and deliberately not taken, because an
unsourced number in a row that carries provenance throughout is worse than a gap.
**What would change it:** thirty minutes on `il-marchio-lardini`, `dalle-origini-a-oggi` and
`evoluzioni-e-numeri`.

## Named by an authority and absent from the map

The Museum at FIT's *Ivy Style* canon names nineteen firms. Five are seated. **Gant is the one that
matters** — New Haven, made shirts for Brooks Brothers and J. Press before selling under its own
name, invented the locker loop, and is a live business. A canon member and a coverage gap.
The rest are defunct, mass-market, or shirt-and-tie houses outside what this map covers: Chipp,
Fenn-Feinstein, The Andover Shop, Bass, Arrow, Van Heusen, Jeffrey Banks, Tommy Hilfiger,
Michael Bastian.

**VAN Jacket** stays out until someone reads Marx's *Ametora*, which is the authority for the
Japanese half of Ivy and has not been opened.
