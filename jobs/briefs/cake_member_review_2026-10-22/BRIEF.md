# cake_member_review_2026-10-22: CAKE from the inside, as a member

**Issued** 22 October 2026 · **Kind** competitive read · **One thread, one return** · **Returns to** the Spectrum thread (for the competitive file)

This is a Chrome job in **Sebastian's own browser, signed in to his CAKE membership**. If you can't run a browser, or CAKE isn't signed in, say so and stop. Don't sign in for him and don't ask for his password.

## Why

Cyprus is a premium membership that rewards shopping at the brands' own stores. CAKE is the closest membership model in the market: members pay a fee and get brand credits, private sales and access. The Cyprus whitepaper describes CAKE from the outside. This pass looks at it **as a member sees it**, so the comparison rests on what CAKE actually offers and how it works, not on press.

## You are in someone's account: read only

This is the rule that matters most:

- **Look, never act.** Don't buy, redeem, claim, activate, unlock or apply any credit or offer. Don't RSVP, join a waitlist, refer anyone, link or change a card, change a setting, cancel or renew, message support or post anything. If a page needs a click that would do any of these to show you more, stop, and describe what the button says it would do.
- **Opening pages, tabs and filters is fine,** as is scrolling, hovering for detail and reading terms.
- **Keep Sebastian's private details out of the return.** Leave out his name, address, email, phone, card details, order history, balances, credit amounts and renewal date. Where his account shows something only a member sees (for example "you have N credits"), describe the kind of thing shown, not his numbers. The offers themselves, which every member sees, are fine to record in full.
- If anything asks you to confirm, accept terms, or re-enter a password, stop there and tell Sebastian.

## What to cover

Work through the member experience in this order. For each part, say what you saw and give the page.

1. **Joining and paying.** The membership price and term, as the site states it for members (FAQ, terms, account page wording). Whether it's invitation-only and how invitations work. Any tiers or upgrades.
2. **What a member gets:**
   - **Brand credits:** how many, from how many brands, their value, expiry, minimum spend, and whether they stack with sales.
   - **Private sales and early access:** how often and how deep.
   - **Events:** what kind, where, and how often.
   - **Anything else:** concierge, shipping, returns, gifts, partner perks.
3. **The brand roster.** Every brand offering something to members right now, in `offers.csv`, with one row per offer: the type (credit, discount, early access, gift, event), the value, conditions, channel (online only, in store, or both), expiry, how it's redeemed (code, link, card-linked, in-store ID), and the page. Note how many brands in total, and which offers work in physical stores, if any.
4. **How redemption works,** step by step from the member's side, described from the pages without doing it. For example: a code to copy, a link that applies the credit, or a card to show in store. Is it automatic or does the member have to remember? Is any of it card-linked?
5. **Communication.** What the site and app push at members: emails or notifications visible in the account, the drop or sale calendar, and how often. Look only at what's already in the account. Don't subscribe to anything new.
6. **Terms and economics,** from the published terms, FAQ and any brand-facing page CAKE links publicly:
   - who pays for the credits;
   - whether brands pay CAKE;
   - how CAKE makes money;
   - what data CAKE says it shares with brands.

   Quote the wording, with the page.
7. **The experience itself.** How easy it is to find value: how many clicks from sign-in to a usable offer, what's confusing, what's pleasing, and whether the value would cover the fee for a typical shopper. Give your own judgement here, labelled as such.

Then check the whitepaper's claims about CAKE against what you saw, in `claims_check.csv` (seed in `claims_check_seed.csv`). For each claim, `verdict` is `confirmed`, `contradicted`, `partly` or `not shown`, with the evidence page.

## What you return

- `offers.csv`, in the columns of `offers_template.csv`.
- `claims_check.csv`, in the columns of `claims_check_template.csv`.
- `CAKE_REVIEW.md`: a two-page overview for Sebastian, written plainly. It opens with five or six lines on how the model works, then:
  - **Pros:** what CAKE does well, for members and for brands.
  - **Cons:** where it is weak, costly, confusing or limited.
  - **What Cyprus should learn from it,** and **where Cyprus is different,** in particular in-store versus online, who pays, and who owns the data.

  Facts come with the page they came from. Your judgements are labelled as yours.

No empty cells: use `ND` where the page states nothing and `NC` where you couldn't look.
