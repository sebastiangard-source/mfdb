# Rules for the dials

Standing rules for the dial evidence section of every seating brief. Sebastian rules on every score. You propose.

## What to record

For **every** dial below, one row in `dials.csv` for each brand: `proposed_score`, the `evidence` (a quotation or a counted fact from the brand's own pages), the `source_url`, and `confidence` (high, medium or low). Tech and craft are covered in their own sections and are not repeated here. Natural fiber, Has stores and Normcore are derived by us and are not scored by you.

- **Read the brand's own pages first.** The men's collection and product pages, the About or Story page, the Journal and the lookbook. A retailer's description is never the brand's, and press counts only where you mark the source as press.
- **Score on the scale printed here.** The scales are the map's. A 1 is a trace and a 0 is none where the dial has a tier. Two-way dials run from -3 to +3.
- **Say what you could not judge.** Write `NC` for the score where the brand's pages give too little to judge, with a note on what you looked at. A guess without a source is worse than an `NC`.
- **Quote and count.** Evidence like "11 of 14 men's polos carry a embroidered logo on the chest" or a quoted line beats a summary. Name how many pages you read.
- **Men's range only.** Score the clothes the men's map shows. Say how you scoped them.
- **No judgments of the brand.** A low score says a register isn't represented. It never says the brand is lesser.

## The dials, with the map's scale and what to read

### comfort
How central softness and physical ease are to what the brand sells — fiber, stretch, drape, wash. 5 = comfort is the entire pitch.

**Read:** Softness, stretch, drape and wash as the brand describes them. Quote the product copy and count the styles that lead with it.

### style
How much the brand trades on design point of view and fashion influence rather than perfected basics. 5 = the look is the reason it exists.

**Read:** Design point of view versus perfected basics. Read the lookbook and the men's collection pages; count how many styles are seasonal statements versus repeat staples.

### fuss
Two-way, measuring what the garment asks of you — not durability, and not fabric technology. Performance fabric usually asks more, not less: cold wash, hang dry, no softener, periodic reproofing. Cotton duck and jersey ask nothing. Left is easy, right is demanding. −3 effortless — hot wash, tumble dry, never press. −1 simple. 0 normal. +1 some care — a specific routine, quick but non-negotiable, where most technical kit sits. +3 specialist care. Derived from each brand's fabric profile, with overrides where fabric cannot see the whole story — Barbour is +3 on re-waxing while scoring nothing on Tech.

**Read:** Read the care line on at least six men's product pages. Quote the instructions (machine wash, dry clean only, re-wax). Scale -3 to +3.

### dur
How long the garment survives being used. Independent of both care and price — a $650 hoodie is no more durable than a $60 one, and heavy cotton outlasts fine wool. 5 = outlives you and is meant to; repairable, and the maker expects you to. 4 = built heavy, expected to last. 3 = solid, ordinary lifespan. 2 = fine gauge or delicate, or built for a season. 0 = not assessed.

**Read:** Weight, construction and repair. Quote any repair program, warranty, gsm or construction statement.

### forg
How closely you can land on your own body. Two things: cut tolerance — whether the garment forgives a body that is not the fit model's — and precision, meaning how finely you can specify. Waist × inseam rather than S/M/L; in-between sizes; separate lengths; slim, classic and relaxed offered in the same size; made-to-measure at the top end. Extended sizing is deliberately excluded: stocking to 5XL is about who can buy the brand at all, which is a different question.

**Read:** Read the size chart and sizing options. Record whether sizes are S/M/L or waist by inseam, whether separate lengths, slim and relaxed fits or made-to-measure exist. Extended sizing does not count.

### denim
How central denim is to the brand's identity. 1 = incidental. 5 = a denim house first — delete the jeans and the company disappears.

**Read:** Share of the men's range that is denim and whether the brand's story is denim.

### cashmere
How central cashmere is to the brand's identity. 1 = incidental. 5 = the company is built on the fiber and wouldn't survive without it.

**Read:** Same, for cashmere.

### linen
How central linen is to the brand's identity. 1 = incidental. 5 = linen is the founding material.

**Read:** Same, for linen.

### cotton
Premium cotton as identity — long-staple fiber, Supima or Sea Island programs, signature jersey. 5 = the brand's reputation rests on its cotton.

**Read:** Premium cotton named as identity (Supima, Sea Island, long-staple, signature jersey).

### wool
Wool as identity — tailoring cloth, merino, Shetland, flannel. 5 = a wool house, with the mills and suiting at the core.

**Read:** Wool as identity (tailoring cloth, merino, Shetland, flannel).

### synth
Performance synthetics and technical blends as identity. 5 = built on engineered fabric.

**Read:** Performance synthetics and technical blends as identity.

### whitet
White tee — how much the brand's plain white T-shirt is a reason to shop it. 1 = sells no plain white tee. 2 = sells one as a basic among many. 3 = the tee is a named, developed product with its own cloth or cut. 4 = a signature — one of the things the brand is known for. 5 = a house built on it: take the tee away and the brand is a different company.

**Read:** Find the plain white tee. Record whether the brand sells one, whether it is named and developed, and whether the brand calls it a signature.

### logo
Logo — how visibly the brand's mark appears on the clothes. 1 = no visible branding. 2 = a small mark on some pieces. 3 = a small mark on most pieces. 4 = a prominent mark on nearly every piece, or large marks on a good share of the range. 5 = the logo is the design — all-over prints, letterforms, monograms.

**Read:** Open at least ten men's product pages and count styles with a visible mark. Say what the mark looks like and where.

### golf
5 = the pro shop is the native habitat. 4 = clubhouse staple. 3 = country-club lifestyle. 2 = owns a golf line or shows up on course. 1 = stopped at the starter's shack.

**Read:** Golf products, golf imagery, golf club partnerships, a golf line.

### golffice
Two-way: where the clothes sit between two dress codes. One end is office and only office (-3); the other is the course and only the course (+3); 0 means they work in both. How much golf runs the brand is Golf, not this.

**Read:** Where the clothes sit between office and course, from the brand's own occasion copy.

### status
Signal power to the audience the wearer is dressing for — loud logos and quiet insider cues both count. 5 = the garment does the talking. 1 = signals nothing, often on purpose.

**Read:** Loud logos and quiet insider cues. Quote what the brand says about who wears it.

### finbro
How central the brand is to the American finance uniform. 5 = the uniform itself — the vest, the quarter-zip. 4 = standard issue at some rank of the career. 1 = has never seen a pitch deck.

**Read:** The finance uniform: vests, quarter-zips, the career wardrobe. Only if the brand's pages go there; otherwise 1.

### ivy
How central to the American campus-trad canon and its lineage. 5 = source material — the campus clothiers and the empire built from them. 4 = the revival's major houses. 1 = no crest, no quad.

**Read:** Campus-trad lineage in the brand's own words or product (button-down oxford, rep stripe, chino, blazer).

### prep
The lifestyle uniform — club decks, coastal weekends, go-to-hell pants. Distinct from Ivy League: Ivy is the collegiate source canon; Prep is the whole way of life. 5 = the uniform itself; 3 = fluent; 1 = never joined the club.

**Read:** Club decks, coastal weekends, the whole way of life. Quote the words and imagery.

### murica
How loudly the brand reads as American — iconography, mythology, and roots. 5 = the flag is practically a material. 1 = another country's story. Actual US manufacturing helps but doesn't decide it.

**Read:** How loudly the brand reads American: iconography, mythology, roots in its own words. Actual US manufacturing helps but does not decide it.

### ital
How much Italy carries the brand — Italian make, Italian mills, Italian design language. An American brand with Italian factories and cloth scores; an Italian brand selling an American fantasy scores lower than its passport suggests. At the top of the scale, Italian make and Italian design are the whole of the brand.

**Read:** Italian make, mills or design language, from product pages and the brand's story.

### fren
How much France carries the brand — Parisian codes, Riviera sun, or Place Vendôme institution; the effortlessness doctrine counts as much as the address. At the top of the scale, French make and French design are the whole of the brand. Mirror cases score both ways: Moncler was born French-alpine and naturalized Italian.

**Read:** French codes, make or address, from the brand's own pages.

### briish
How much Britain carries the brand — the mills, the shop lineage, the country-and-club wardrobe, or London's design schools. A Neapolitan house softening an English jacket scores; a London address selling something else does not. At the top of the scale Britain is the whole of the brand; in the middle the brand speaks the register fluently; at the bottom there is a trace.

**Read:** British mills, shop lineage, country wardrobe or London design.

### bruv
Two-way: which street the brand speaks from. One end is British (terrace, mod, casuals, the check); the other is American (New York and Los Angeles). Score from -3 (British) to +3 (American); 0 means neither.

**Read:** Which street the brand speaks from.

### scandi
How much the brand belongs to the Nordic design tradition — restraint, function, a muted palette, democratic design. Like the Italian and French registers it is geographic: Nordic origin puts a brand on this scale, and its place on the scale is how much of the tradition it actually trades in. A Swedish house selling English shirt formality scores low; a non-Nordic house working the same register scores at most a 2, because the scale is about where a brand comes from as well as how it designs. At the top the tradition is the whole of the brand; in the middle the brand is fluent in it; at the bottom there is a trace.

**Read:** Nordic origin and design tradition; non-Nordic brands score at most 2.

### street
How native the brand is to street culture — sneakers, drops, skate and hip-hop lineage. 5 = the street is the source; 4 = fluent; 3 = adopted by the street, or borrowing from it; 1 = never touched pavement.

**Read:** Sneakers, drops, skate and hip-hop lineage.

### avant
How far the brand pushes against clothing's conventions — silhouette, construction, concept, presentation. 5 = an ongoing argument with clothing itself; 4 = radical form you can wear to dinner; 3 = a foot in the gallery; 1 = clothes as clothes.

**Read:** Silhouette, construction and concept that argue with clothing.

### weird
How gleefully strange the design output is — pigeon clutches, tabi toes, jackets for Mars. Distinct from Avant-Garde: avant argues with clothing; weird just delights in oddity. 5 = the odd is the brand; 3 = a streak of strange; 1 = plays it straight.

**Read:** Oddity as the design output.

### boat
Which deck the clothes are cut for, by boat length. 1 = a 25ft center console you drive yourself, cooler in the bow. 2 = a 35-40ft weekender, you know the lines. 3 = 50-60ft, blazer at the club afterward. 4 = 80-120ft crewed, linen and no visible logos. 5 = 200ft+, cashmere on deck in July. 0 = no boat. Unlike the other registers this is a tier, not an adoption score — it asks which deck, not how nautical.

**Read:** The deck the clothes are cut for, by boat length. 0 if there is no boat.

### sail
Two-way: which kind of boating. One end is under sail, kit for working a boat (-3); the other is under power, a deck for guests (+3). How big the boat is, is Boat. Score only where the brand has a boating register; otherwise NC.

**Read:** Sail versus power, only where a boating register exists.

### racquet
Which court the clothes are cut for, least to most fancy. 1 = pickleball, public court. 2 = municipal tennis or padel, booked online. 3 = country-club tennis. 4 = private club grass or clay, whites enforced. 5 = squash, members-only — the court is a building, not a surface, so a club owns it. 0 = no court. Like Boat this is a tier, not an adoption score.

**Read:** The court the clothes are cut for. 0 if none.

### ski
How much the mountain runs the brand. 5 = the slope is the founding brief and still current. 4 = a serious ski program inside a broader technical house. 3 = a real ski line rather than a capsule. 2 = a capsule, an après register, or a historic association. 1 = a trace. 0 = no mountain. Unlike Boat and Racquet this is an adoption score, not a tier — it asks how much, not which slope.

**Read:** The mountain's place in the brand. 0 if none.

### western
Western — How much the brand belongs to Western wear — boots, pearl-snap shirts, ranch denim, the rodeo — from a boot-maker down to one Western shirt in the range. Seeded from the ranges as they read on each brand's site; a note on each record says so.

**Read:** Boots, pearl-snap shirts, ranch denim, the rodeo.

### rock
Rock — How much the brand dresses the stage and the road — leather, black, biker and greaser cuts — from a rock house down to a single leather jacket. Seeded from the ranges as they read on each brand's site; a note on each record says so.

**Read:** Leather, black, biker and greaser cuts, the stage and the road.

### workwear
Workwear — How much the clothes descend from a trade — duck canvas, riveted denim, chore coats, tin cloth — from a company still selling work clothes down to a chore jacket in the range. Seeded from the ranges as they read on each brand's site; a note on each record says so.

**Read:** Descent from a trade (duck canvas, riveted denim, chore coats, tin cloth). Count the pieces.

### outdoor
Outdoor — How much the brand is technical outdoor clothing worn as everyday dress — shells, fleece, trail shoes on city streets — from a mountain house down to a fleece in the range. Seeded from the ranges as they read on each brand's site; a note on each record says so.

**Read:** Technical outdoor clothing worn as everyday dress.

### surf
Surf — How much the beach runs the brand — boardshorts, washed cotton, salt-water ease — from a surfer's house down to a swim short in the range. Seeded from the ranges as they read on each brand's site; a note on each record says so.

**Read:** Boardshorts, washed cotton, the beach as the brand's world.

### shoes
How central footwear is to the brand, not how good the shoes are. 0 = no shoes at all. 5 = a shoemaker first.

**Read:** Count the footwear styles against clothing styles on the men's pages, and say how the brand describes its shoes.
